# Ten Halo CE maps, remastered in Campaign Evolved's look

**Author:** MJOLNIR Core
**Summary:** Nine classic maps and Yoyorast Island now look like they belong in Campaign Evolved: real terrain and grass, the game's own rock and trees, a living ocean and rivers, and Halo CE's lamps lit by Unreal. Drag the pictures to compare, then see how it was built and where the code lives.
**Tags:** multiplayer, maps, lighting

![Death Island's beach, remastered](/blog-images/ce-maps-remastered/death-island-beach.jpg?before=/blog-images/ce-maps-remastered/death-island-beach-classic.jpg)

The classic maps came to Halo Campaign Evolved looking like Halo CE, and
[lighting them with Unreal Engine](/blog/lighting-the-classics) kept them that
way: the same surfaces, the same colours, real light. This update goes
further on nine of them and on Yoyorast Island. Their ground, rock, grass, trees and water are now
drawn the way Campaign Evolved draws its own levels. The layout and every
weapon, vehicle and flag spawn stay exactly where Halo CE put them. The new
rocks and tree trunks are solid where they stand, as Halo CE's boulders and
trees were.

The ten are Blood Gulch, Death Island, Ice Fields, Sidewinder, Timberland,
Infinity, Gephyrophobia, Battle Creek and Rat Race, plus the Custom Edition
community's Yoyorast Island.

Every comparison below is the current hub version of a map (left) against
the remastered one (right), from the same camera. Drag the divider to compare.

## What changed

- **Real terrain.** The ground under each outdoor map is rebuilt as an
  Unreal Engine landscape from Halo CE's own heights. Sand turns to earth
  and gravel inland, banks go green as they steepen, and the beaches get
  ripples you can see from eye level.
- **Grass that grows.** Several kinds of grass, weeds and tufts, at
  different heights, on the ground Halo CE painted green, moving in the
  wind. Pebbles and driftwood scatter where it painted sand. Nothing grows
  under a roof, where it would only stand in the dark.
- **Campaign Evolved's rock and trees.** Cliffs are drawn in the game's own
  layered rock with real depth, instead of one tiled texture, and the
  campaign's cliff stones are set into the walls. Halo CE's boulders and
  trees become the campaign's own, sized to match.
- **Water that moves.** Death Island and Yoyorast sit in a real ocean that
  swells and breaks on the sand, over a sea floor that falls away from the
  shore. Timberland's and Battle Creek's streams are now rivers that run
  along their beds and over their falls.
- **Tracks in the sand.** Drive a Warthog across a beach and it leaves tyre
  tracks, as the campaign's own beaches do. Footprints follow you too.
- **Halo CE's lamps, lit by Unreal.** The light fixtures Halo CE placed now
  cast real light and shadow.
- **Snow.** Ice Fields, Sidewinder and Gephyrophobia get snow-covered ground
  and rock, and Ice Fields and Sidewinder get falling snow.
- **Metal.** The metal the bases share has the game's Forerunner finish:
  fine scratches and wear that catch the light, without changing Halo CE's
  colours.
- **Life.** Birds over the cliffs, gulls along the shore, falling leaves and
  wind.

## How it was made

None of this is placed by hand. Each map goes through one pipeline, from the
converted Halo CE map to a finished map pack, and running it again after a
change rebuilds the map exactly. A map's entry in one config file says
which of its shaders are ground and which are cliffs, where its rivers run
and what its sea does. Everything below is driven from there.

### From triangles to a heightmap

Halo CE has no terrain system. Blood Gulch's ground is a few thousand
triangles in the same mesh as its bases and cliffs, painted with blended
textures and Bungie's baked light. Campaign Evolved's beaches are Unreal
Engine landscapes: a grid of heights that gets grass, footprints, tyre
tracks and the game's own ground materials.

So the first step samples Halo CE's ground onto a regular grid, one height
every metre over Blood Gulch, and writes it out as a 16-bit heightmap.
Where two layers of ground overlap, such as a plateau whose lip overhangs
the cliff below, the grid takes the lower one. Wherever the grid can't
follow the shape (cliffs, overhangs, bases, steps of more than two metres
between neighbouring samples), the landscape is cut away and Halo CE's own
mesh draws that spot instead. In the picture below the dark areas are cut
away, and you can still see Bungie's triangles in the shading.

![Blood Gulch's heightmap](/blog-images/ce-maps-remastered/blood-gulch-heightmap.jpg)

The landscape's material is Campaign Evolved's own: Megascans sand, earth,
gravel and grass, blended by what Halo CE painted at each point and by the
slope. It also reads a top-down bake of Halo CE's light, so the shadows of
the cliffs and bases fall exactly where they did in 2001.

### Procedural grass, pebbles and wall rock

The grass, weeds, pebbles, driftwood and the rocks set into the cliffs are
placed by a procedural generator we wrote for this, which runs when the map
is built. Its rules come from Campaign Evolved itself: we read the B30
beach's placements straight out of the game's World Partition cells and
copied its densities, sizes and heights above the sea.

For each rule, the generator:

1. **Asks Halo CE what the ground is.** Halo CE's ground shaders blend two
   detail textures, such as sand and grass, by the base texture's alpha.
   The generator works that blend out at every point, so it knows how much
   grass and how much sand is there. Grass grows where Halo CE drew grass,
   pebbles lie where it drew sand, and tufts line the edge between them.
2. **Clusters it.** A smooth noise field decides where clumps gather and
   where the ground stays bare, so the grass grows in patches rather than as
   an even carpet.
3. **Throws out what doesn't fit.** Points on slopes too steep for the rule
   are dropped, and so are points under a roof, so nothing grows in a
   tunnel. Nothing tall goes where players walk. Halo CE's simulation knows
   only Halo CE's collision, so a knee-high rock would be something to walk
   through.
4. **Seeds everything.** Every point is random but seeded, so every PC
   places the same blade of grass in the same spot. Your opponent sees the
   tuft you are hiding behind.

The rocks in the cliffs work the other way round. The generator walks the
cliff faces, picks spots where a rock would sit against the wall, and lights
each rock with the Halo CE light on the wall behind it, so a rock in shade
stays in shade.

The result is one file of instances that the level loader spawns as
instanced meshes when the map loads: almost 300,000 on Blood Gulch alone.

![Blood Gulch's procedural scatter](/blog-images/ce-maps-remastered/blood-gulch-scatter.jpg)

Halo CE's own boulders and trees are swapped rather than scattered. Each
tree is rebuilt from a real tree in B30's cells, with its trunk and canopy
pieces kept in the same arrangement, and each boulder is sized to the
Halo CE model it replaces. Their collision is merged into the map's own, so
they are solid in game.

### Rock with real depth

The cliffs use a material of our own built on the game's rock. It projects
the campaign's jagged Megascans rock from three directions, so it never
stretches the way Halo CE's 1024 x 256 cliff strip did over a twenty-metre
wall. Halo CE's own colours come through as banding, and its baked light
works as occlusion, so the clefts stay dark.

The cliff walls are then rebuilt as a Nanite mesh and tessellated, so the
rock's height map pushes the surface in and out for real. Halo CE's map
geometry splits every vertex where two faces meet, and moving those apart
would open a crack along every edge, so all the vertices at one point are
welded to a single normal first. The displacement fades out toward the
edges where a wall meets the ground. Rat Race's tunnels are lit by Halo CE's
lightmap instead, since no sun reaches them.

### Simulated water

The ocean and rivers are Fluid Flux, the shallow-water simulation Campaign
Evolved uses for its own rivers and beaches, running live in the map.

- **The ocean.** The landscape's floor shelves away from the shore under the
  sea, so the waves have something to break on, and an apron of shallows
  around the structures standing in the water lets the waves feel them.
  Splashes run along the waterline. A mask keeps the sea and its fog out of
  the tunnels that dip below sea level.
- **Rivers.** The simulation sees the ground from above, so under an
  overhang it would take the rock's top for the river bed. Each river gets a
  bed of its own that only the simulation sees: the lowest surface at every
  point inside the river's banks, and walls it can't climb outside them.

Death Island's heightmap shows the new sea floor: the blue around the island
is ground Halo CE never had, shelving down from the beach so the surf has
somewhere to break.

![Death Island's heightmap, with its new sea floor](/blog-images/ce-maps-remastered/death-island-heightmap.jpg)

### Halo CE's lamps, in Unreal

Halo CE placed its light fixtures in the map data. Each one becomes an
Unreal point light in the same place, with the same colour and strength,
stepped a little out of its fixture so the fixture doesn't shade its own
lamp. Halo CE's walls are one-sided, and the sun leaked through every inside
corner. So a copy of every wall, set slightly behind it, casts the shadows.

## Getting it

The remastered maps arrive as an ordinary update to each map. Update the
launcher first, then update the ten maps in it. The remastered maps carry
their grass and decal files beside the level, and they need CE runtime pack
1.8.0, which the updated launcher installs for you. Maps downloaded in game
update the same way.

The other ten classic maps are unchanged. They are mostly indoor or built
maps, with little ground or rock to remaster.
